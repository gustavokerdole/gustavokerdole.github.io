My formation in France is a double-degree program with my home university in Brazil, Unicamp, where I have already finished the third year of Bachelor of Science in Physics.

Research Interests

My research interests lie in quantum computing, with a focus on quantum algorithms, quantum software, and the development of practical quantum technologies. I am particularly interested in variational quantum algorithms, quantum optimization, quantum simulation, and the mathematical foundations of algorithms such as Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT).

I am also interested in quantum error correction and fault-tolerant quantum computing, as well as the challenges involved in scaling current noisy quantum devices. I have experience developing quantum circuits using Qiskit and PennyLane, working with IBM quantum hardware, variational algorithms, and error mitigation techniques. My applications of interest include optimization, quantum chemistry, financial modeling, and scientific computing.

  • Quantum algorithms and quantum software
  • Variational quantum algorithms and optimization
  • Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT)
  • Quantum error correction and fault-tolerant quantum computing
  • Quantum simulation and quantum chemistry
  • Superconducting quantum architectures

Research Projects

Improved quantum algorithm design using polynomial optimization

July 2026 — August 2026

This project investigates the application of polynomial optimization techniques, particularly semidefinite programming (SDP), within the framework of the Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT). QSVT provides a unified approach for describing many quantum algorithms, including Grover's algorithm, Shor's algorithm, and Hamiltonian simulation, by reducing algorithm design to the manipulation of quantum-implemented polynomials. The project focuses on understanding how optimized polynomial selection can improve the performance of QSVT-based quantum algorithms, exploring current theoretical developments, extending these techniques to new examples, and analyzing their applications in quantum circuit design.

Quantum Energy Advantage in Max-Cut Optimization: A Break-Even Analysis

April 2026 — July 2026

This project investigated the potential energy advantage of quantum computing by developing an analytical framework to compare the energy-to-solution scaling of classical, NISQ, and fault-tolerant quantum computing architectures. The work modeled the impact of algorithmic complexity, error mitigation overhead, cryogenic infrastructure, and error correction costs to identify the conditions under which quantum systems could outperform classical approaches in terms of energy efficiency.

What can be learned from 2 qubits?

August 2024 — July 2025

This project explored fundamental concepts in Quantum Information Theory through the study of two-qubit systems and their applications. The project covered the mathematical description of quantum states using density operators and generalized measurements (POVMs), quantum evolution, and quantum channels, including the effects of noise and purification techniques. Key topics in quantum foundations were investigated, including entanglement characterization through Schmidt decomposition, Bell inequalities, and the CHSH scenario. The project also explored quantum algorithms, such as Deutsch's algorithm and the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), as well as quantum communication protocols including BB84 and E91, providing a broad introduction to the theoretical foundations underlying quantum technologies.

Neutron stars and black hole models

August 2023 — July 2024

This project focused on the study of black holes and neutron stars within the framework of Einstein's general relativity. The work involved studying spacetime geometry, geodesic motion, tensor calculus, and Einstein's field equations and their solutions. Topics investigated included gravitational waves, stellar models, and black hole physics, with particular emphasis on Hawking radiation and black hole evaporation.
